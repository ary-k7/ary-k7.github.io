Equity momentum and asset rotation in India

Momentum and trend-rotation strategies on Indian stocks, market-cap segments, gold and cash, tested over eight to nineteen years with survivorship, corporate-action and timing checks. Most of the edge is drawdown control.

The question

Does cross-sectional price momentum beat the Indian index after costs, and does a simple trend rule across equities, gold and cash help? If it does, which part is real and which part is curve-fitting?

The answer from the backtests: momentum beat the index in each version written up here, but the holdout results are well below the development results, and in the strongest version gold did a lot of the work.

What I did

  • Nifty100 rotation. Top 20 stocks by trailing return, equal weight, with a sweep of four lookbacks against four rebalance periods to check for a plateau rather than a peak.
  • Futures rotation with stops. Monthly rotation on a 210-stock F&O universe with four stop versions: none, fixed, trailing and volatility-adjusted. The 1-minute data was not adjusted for splits and bonuses, so the engine adjusts for them automatically: 45 ex-dates corrected, and an ambiguous band of gaps left for manual review, because a real 20% limit-down can look like a bonus.
  • Regime-filtered momentum. Risk-adjusted momentum with a Nifty volatility-and-trend filter, parking idle money in gold and liquid ETFs. It was built with the AI-agent research loop on a point-in-time F&O universe, then rebuilt independently, with no shared code, on a point-in-time Nifty 500.
  • Rotation rules. Nifty, gold and cash on relative strength, and large, mid and small caps with a move to cash when all three are falling, checked with a walk-forward.

  • The futures rotation with a volatility-adjusted stop was the best of the four stop versions (backtest, 102 months to Jul 2026, flat 0.5% a month carry and no other costs, monthly Sharpe 1.19). Correcting splits and bonuses raised its total result by about a fifth, so the raw numbers had been biased low.
  • On development data, all 36 neighbouring regime settings had a Sharpe of about 1.4 or more, which is the plateau I wanted to see.
  • Out of sample (2012–2026) the market-cap walk-forward made 14.2% a year against 12.8% for the Nifty 500: about 95% of the in-sample return, but a much smaller margin over the index. The edge is drawdown control: it beat the benchmark in only about 60% of rolling 12-month windows and lost in 2018–19 and 2022–23.

What would break this

  • Survivorship. The Nifty100 and futures versions use today’s index members. The Nifty 500 rebuild is missing 118 delisted names.
  • Gold. In the regime-filtered version, gold and liquid ETFs earned more than stocks over ten years. Part of the late return is luck on gold, not stock selection.
  • A used holdout. It was looked at three times, and about 100 regime variants were tried on development data.
  • Parameter choice. The Nifty100 parameters were picked on the full window, and the market-cap rule’s 3-month lookback is a spike, not a plateau.
  • Not modelled. Taxes, which matter for monthly rotation, and real futures roll costs. The Nifty, gold and cash rotation is gross of costs and starts at the 2008 low, which flatters it, so I have left its figures out.

Non-expiry days in index options

Intraday option selling on the Nifty session before weekly expiry, plus three related Sensex and overnight-carry books I tested and chose not to run.

The question

Expiry comes just twice a week, so an expiry-only book can lose a month on one session. I added the day before expiry (1DTE): more time value is left, so theta is slower and gamma milder, and it fails on a trending session rather than an expiry-afternoon gamma move. Does that day carry its own edge, and does holding into expiry morning add to it?

What I did

  • Built seven intraday Nifty 1DTE variants in three families: decay-triggered short straddles and strangles, fades of a late range break, and fixed-time afternoon strangles, all with premium stops.
  • Backtested 252 1DTE days (Jun 2021 – Jul 2026) on 1-minute broker data, and kept 12 closing-auction-era days (Aug – Oct 2026) out of every search.
  • Ran 4,266 backtests of the risk rules and cross-validated the variant mix in four folds.
  • Searched three related books: Sensex 1DTE intraday (6,463 configs) and hedged overnight carry on Sensex (50,000) and Nifty (40,000).
  • I set the targets, constraints and acceptance rules; AI coding agents wrote most of the code and ran the searches.

What I found

Nifty 1DTE book, backtest, 252 days (Jun 2021 – Jul 2026), statutory charges, no slippage, hedge wings excluded:

Measure Value
Winning days 68.3%
Sharpe 3.58
Average win to average loss 2.05
Max drawdown, as a share of average annual net 14%
Worst synthetic 1–2% jump day, as a share of average annual net 22%

That Sharpe assumes free fills and no tail event. At about 21 orders a day, 0.5 point of slippage per fill removes about 28% of annual net; 1 point removes about 55%. The single best setting per family scored Sharpe 3.0 in-sample and 1.45 out of fold, hence several differently timed variants. On the 12 closing-auction-era days the book won 10, but its worst day there was slightly worse than the worst in the 252-day backtest.

The other three books failed. Sensex overnight won 74.8% of 155 backtest nights (Jul 2023 – Jul 2026, wings included, no slippage) until I found bad expiry-morning opening option prints on 142 of 163 nights, which let the simulator fill gap-through stops at the stop. Repriced, its return falls about 70% or turns negative. Under a gap-loss cap, the best Nifty overnight book reached about a quarter of its return target. Sensex intraday failed its holdout twice after the expiry weekday moved.

What would break this

  • Costs. One point per fill takes over half the net.
  • The tail. A Sharpe of 3.58 from a cost-free short-premium backtest mostly says the bad day has not come.
  • Selection. The holdout days were reused across five 1DTE searches. Two variants passed a holdout, one on its third look, one exactly at the bar; four have no clean test.
  • Execution. Four entry signals are unconfirmed on an execution platform, and overall stops fill inside the minute.

What I would do next

Measure slippage from bid-ask data, confirm the entry signals, and judge the book on forward weeks, not the reused holdout.

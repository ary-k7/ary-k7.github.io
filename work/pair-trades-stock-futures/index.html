Pair trades in stock futures

Long one stock, short its same-sector rival in futures; five systematic versions failed their backtest targets, so the pairs I trade are picked on a thesis and tracked as a basket.

The question

Can a long/short leg in two same-sector rivals add return that does not depend on the index?

The rest of my book sells index options and holds momentum futures, so a sharp index move hits both. A pair holds equal rupees long and short in two rivals, so the index largely cancels and the result rests on one company beating the other. I wanted to know whether a rule could pick these trades.

What I did

  • Built a list of 63 same-sector rival pairs, with both stocks required to have futures on the signal date (point-in-time F&O membership).
  • Tested five systematic families on daily NSE prices, the last on a point-in-time universe of correlated same-sector pairs.
  • Every run used train 2016–20, validation 2021–23, 10 bps per side per leg, and a frozen brief with targets set before the search.
  • I set the targets, constraints and acceptance rules; AI coding agents wrote most of the code and ran the searches.
  • Discarded the first ratio-stop run after finding two accounting bugs in its judge.

What I found

Backtest, train 2016–20 and validation 2021–23, 10 bps per side per leg:

Family Variants Result
Ratio breakout, stop-only exit 66 Target missed. Market-neutral version negative in validation in every variant (−1.37 to −0.61 Sharpe)
Ratio trend, trailing exit 101 Every variant loses in both eras; train Sharpe −1.6 to −0.48, validation −2.0 to −0.87
Results-season reaction 24 23 of 24 negative in train; none passes both eras
Ten-pair breakout book 48 0 of 48 pass; best is train +0.05, validation +0.77
Same book, systematic universe 24 0 of 24 pass; every variant loses in train

The trend rule lost before costs as well (gross Sharpe −0.49 for the least-bad variant), so rival-pair ratios tended to reverse at these horizons. The only positive ratio-breakout version let the winning leg grow without rebalancing, which is pyramiding with uncapped risk: Maruti/M&M lost 114% of notional while its ratio never reached the stop. On the systematic universe, 2020 alone sank the train period, and an accidental look at the stale holdout showed 2025 losing too: a dispersion regime, not a stable edge.

A mirror rule, fading ratio trends, is drafted but not run. It was formed after seeing the losses, so only forward data can test it.

What survived is thesis-driven: eight pairs from eight sectors, from private banks to two-wheelers, each where recent results and news favoured the long stock, tracked as an equal-weight basket.

What would break this

  • Hindsight. The basket’s pairs were chosen knowing which leg won.
  • Stale holdout. The 2024–26 holdout was opened early in the research, so it can filter ideas but not confirm them.
  • Unmodelled costs. The basket uses price returns only: no brokerage, taxes, dividends, futures carry or borrow cost.
  • Selection. 263 variants searched on the same 2016–23 data raise the chance that any positive cell is luck.

What I would do next

Track the basket forward from a fixed date with costs, and run the fade rule only on data after 23 Oct 2026.

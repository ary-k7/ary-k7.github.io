Expiry-day volatility premium: crude oil, natural gas and Nifty

Whether options on MCX commodities and Nifty are priced above the moves that follow on expiry day. Yes for crude oil and Nifty, no for natural gas; on crude, only a delta-hedged trade keeps a significant share.

The question

On MCX expiry days after 18:00, are options priced above the moves that follow, is there any directional edge, and can a crude-oil selling book survive costs? I then ran the same tests on Nifty.

The answer: crude oil has a clear variance premium, but it is only worth having with a delta hedge. Natural gas has almost none. There is no directional edge in any of the four commodities.

What I did

  • Used 1-minute MCX futures and options from broker data: 46 crude expiries, Jan 2023 to Oct 2026.
  • Compared Black-76 implied volatility on the at-the-money legs with realised volatility from 1-minute futures returns. Variance premium is the mean of (IV² − RV²) / IV².
  • Simulated a short straddle hedged with futures every 5 minutes, net of modelled slippage on every option fill and hedge trade, and compared it with the unhedged trade.
  • Ran 32 directional correlation tests (drift, momentum, reversal, opening range) across four commodities and two samples, plus a pinning test; one or two should pass by chance.
  • Ran the same scripts on 159 Nifty expiry days (Nov 2023 – Nov 2026), aligned on minutes to the close.
  • For the crude book, searched 3,264 premium-stop setups on 23 training expiries, then ran validation, holdout, plateau, delay, bootstrap, shock and slippage tests.

What I found

Backtest, entry at 18:00, Jan 2023 – Oct 2026 (46 expiries; 41 for gold mini), net of assumed slippage per option/futures fill of 0.3/1 point (crude), 0.05/0.10 (gas) and 5/5 (gold mini). Gold-mini fill costs are the least certain of the three:

Measure Crude oil Natural gas Gold mini
Realised ÷ implied vol (median) 0.81 0.91 0.78
Variance premium (t) 31% (7.8) 3% (0.4) 26% (3.0)
Hedged short straddle, net % of premium (t) 12.0% (3.7) −7.3% (−1.9) 13.1% (2.4)
Unhedged, net % of premium (t) 6.5% (0.7) 0.8% (0.1) 9.7% (0.8)
Unhedged worst day, % of premium −142% −133% −240%

The crude premium was positive every year, but the hedged trade kept only 5–6% of premium in 2023–24 against 18–20% in 2025–26. Unhedged, one trending US session can erase a year of it.

Nifty is far cheaper to trade. Its straddle is 0.46% of the index against 2.32% for gas, but one tick on four fills costs 0.18% of the Nifty credit against 3.85% for gas. In backtest (Nifty Nov 2023 – Nov 2026, gas Jan 2023 – Oct 2026, after hedging costs), a delta-hedged Nifty straddle sold at 10:00 kept 12.2% of credit (t 3.7); the same trade on gas lost 6.3% (t −1.4). Nifty’s variance-premium t-statistic rose from 1.1 in 2024 to 3.6 in 2025 and 4.6 in 2026.

The crude book (backtest, 46 expiries, statutory charges plus an assumed 0.30 point slippage per fill) has a Sharpe of 3.62, about 1.7% a year on its sizing capital. Slippage sensitivity: Sharpe 3.07 at 0.5 point, 2.75 at 0.75, 2.20 at 1 point.

What would break this

  • Sample size. 46 expiries. The family Deflated Sharpe is 0.37, so the crude edge is not statistically proven.
  • A stale holdout. It had been opened in earlier runs; only expiries after 15 Oct 2026 can confirm the book.
  • Regime dependence. Most of the crude and Nifty premium comes from 2025–26. No unhedged Nifty setting clears t 2 in both halves of the sample, and at 1 point per fill none clears it at all.
  • Assumed costs. Slippage is modelled, not measured, and the margin is modelled, not a broker quote.
  • Neighbours that failed. The natural-gas book failed its holdout, the gold-mini book stays a paper-trade candidate (holdout Sharpe 1.43 against a 2.0 target), and a four-commodity basket failed its holdout.

What I would do next

Measure real fill costs on crude, then judge the book only on expiries it has not yet seen.

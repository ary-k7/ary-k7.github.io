The question

Everything else in my book is short-dated options that expire within the month. This leg is the opposite. It is long-only, fundamental and held for months, with research written for a three-to-five-year view. Its risks are earnings, balance sheets and patience rather than gamma.

The capital is student money raised by the fund. I have been Head of the Investment Cell since June 2026. The question is how a student team can run that money with a process that holds up.

What I did

  • I lead the institute’s first student-run investment team, 10+ members, and designed the sector-wise capital allocation frameworks we use.
  • I divided the team into pods, each accountable for its own ideas and results. It makes ownership clear and puts some competitive spirit into the team.
  • I took charge of risk management and set the risk limits at pod level and at fund level.
  • We evaluate 500+ stocks, combining top-down work on macro trends and sector dynamics with bottom-up company analysis. We look for durable moats and strong balance sheets, and weight positions by conviction.
  • After the maiden year we added a pre-investment checklist that tests whether an idea’s expected payoff horizon fits the fund’s remaining life, together with tighter position sizing and segment-level limits.

How the fund works

  • Mandate. About 80% of the fund goes to fundamentals-based investing in equities, debt and commodities; the other 20% to a rules-based, back-tested F&O sleeve with automated risk limits. The benchmark is the Nifty 500.
  • Structure. The fund is a registered partnership. The partners are the Fund Manager, the Head of the Investment Cell and a partner elected from the incoming first-year cohort, so that the fund passes from one batch to the next.
  • Capital. Contributions come only from the PGP, FABM, PGPX and PhD cohorts, as loans carrying a floating rate linked to the fund’s performance. Members of the investment team contribute too. There is no management fee; taxes and brokerage are charged at cost.
  • Reporting. Monthly performance disclosure to every investor by email, quarterly townhalls, and macro notes such as the Macro note on the US midterms, crude and Indian markets.

What would break this

  • Track record. The fund is in its second year and pays out at year-end, so there is no long record to judge the process by. No performance figures are shown here.
  • Duration. Research built for years runs inside a fund that lasts months. Our first-year review called it a mismatch.
  • Scale. Capital is small; the lessons may not carry to a larger book.
  • People. The analysts are part-time students, and the team turns over every year.

What I would do next

Apply the horizon check to every new idea, keep segment limits tight, and publish investment theses alongside the monthly disclosures so the process can be judged before the returns can.

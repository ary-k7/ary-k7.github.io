Positional weekly Nifty options

A weekly Nifty options sleeve carried overnight, mostly short premium with an option-buying hedge, backtested over 278 expiry windows and locked for a forward check.

The question

My expiry-day books live and die on one session’s gamma. Positions carried through the week collect theta over several sessions, so the last afternoon matters less; the price is overnight gap risk, which stops do not cover. Does a weekly sleeve earn on its own and move little with the intraday books, its bad days coming from gaps rather than expiry afternoons?

What I did

  • Built eight weekly Nifty strategies in four families: short straddles and strangles, dynamic-skew strangles, directional short options against a range break, and overnight option buying on premium momentum.
  • Backtested 278 weekly windows on 1-minute broker data, filling gap-through stops at the worst of the stop and the first prints, on a random-month split (34 train, 15 validation, 15 holdout).
  • Searched 2,685 configs against a frozen brief: every one-step neighbour profitable, and profitable in 5 of 6 years and both data eras.
  • I set the targets, constraints and acceptance rules; AI coding agents wrote most of the code and ran the searches.
  • Locked the book for a 13-expiry forward check, due about late January 2027.

What I found

Backtest, 278 windows (Jul 2021 – Oct 2026), statutory charges plus 0.5 point slippage per fill, wings included where used:

Measure Value
Weeks won 69%
Sharpe, weekly annualised (train / val / holdout) 3.15 (2.91 / 3.10 / 2.71)
Share of net earned overnight 44%
Max drawdown, as a share of average annual net about 26%
Net at 1 point slippage, as a share of cost-free net 81%
Daily correlation with the intraday expiry book 0.12

Every calendar year was positive. On the intraday book’s 20 worst days the sleeve made money overall, losing on 8.

The search found no robust monthly strategy. My monthly entry day was an isolated peak: on the day I actually traded, those strategies earned about 40% less and lost in the holdout, so I dropped them. Sharpe went from 2.69 to 2.88 with a new weekly strangle, and to 3.15 with the option-buying pair, whose weekly correlation with the short-premium strategies is −0.16 to −0.31.

What would break this

  • Gaps. Nothing caps a gap. One strategy has wings; the new strangle has no stop, and wings cut its edge to under 1% of the naked version.
  • The tail. A Sharpe of 3.15 on mostly short premium says the larger gap has not yet come.
  • Selection. The holdout is stale (opened seven times by five other runs) and every change saw the full history. Deflated Sharpe, for 1,291 effective trials, is 0.60 for the book and 0.006 for the new strangle. The option-buying pair failed its validation Sharpe bar (0.6–0.87 against 0.8); I added it because it hedged the worst weeks. It lost over the last six weeks of data.
  • Regime. The new strangle earned about 2.2 times as much per month in the earlier data era as the later and is negative in 2026.
  • Execution. Buying straight after a sharp premium rise can fill worse than the backtest.

What I would do next

Let the forward check reach 13 expiries before changing anything, and measure fills on the option buys.

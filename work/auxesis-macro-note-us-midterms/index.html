Three ballots, one barrel: the US midterms, crude and Indian markets

A macro note I co-drafted for Auxesis Capital's shareholders ahead of the 3 November US midterms. Its argument is that India is a crude-and-rates trade first and an election trade second.

This is Macro Note No. 3 of Auxesis Capital, the student-run investment fund of IIM Ahmedabad’s finance club, dated 3 November 2026. I co-drafted it with a fellow member of the fund, whose byline it carries. Read the full note (PDF).

The question

Three elections landed within days of each other: the US midterms, a hung Knesset in Israel and Lula’s win in Brazil. The fund’s shareholders wanted to know what any of it meant for the Nifty.

Our answer was that the elections matter to India mainly through one question: whether the Iran war restarts before the new US Congress sits in January. The chain runs from the election outcome to the odds of renewed strikes, then to Brent, then to the rupee, bond yields and foreign flows.

What we did

  • Explained the US midterms through their closest Indian analogues, so that a reader who follows Lok Sabha and Rajya Sabha arithmetic could see why a Democratic House controls the purse but cannot pass laws over a veto.
  • Set prediction-market odds beside their recent misses in Brazil and Israel, to show how much weight they can carry.
  • Built an India dashboard of the Nifty, the rupee, the 10-year G-sec, foreign portfolio flows, Brent and US yields.
  • Laid out the four things most likely to move the Nifty over six to eight weeks, in order: Iran in the lame-duck window, the Fed and US yields, a Senate surprise, and contagion from Brazil.
  • Mapped six groups of Indian sectors against two outcomes, a truce and a lame-duck strike.
  • Wrote three scenarios for India, each with an indicative Brent range, a four-to-eight-week read and a 2027 view.

What we said

  • The midterm result was mostly priced; the Iran war was not. Prediction markets had the ceasefire holding through 15 November at about a coin flip, and that curve mattered more for Indian equities than the House result.
  • The biggest US rally is not the best outcome for India. Yields and Iran matter more than the move in the S&P 500, so a Republican sweep could leave India lagging.
  • Our base case was a risk-off phase through January followed by a more durable relief rally. The next six to eight weeks carried event risk, while the 2027 picture improved under almost every election outcome.
  • India had been a relative haven in October because it has little weight in the AI trade. We flagged that a disorderly AI drawdown would still hurt the rupee through the dollar and global risk aversion.

What would break this

  • Second-hand inputs. The US scenario odds and reaction ranges came from a bank’s note as reported elsewhere, and the note says to treat them as indicative.
  • Prediction markets. They are thin, a few large accounts can move them, and the note itself documents two large misses in the ten days before it was written.
  • Unconfirmed reports. Several inputs, including a reported request for strikes after the midterms, rested on a single source. The note marks each one.
  • Judgement, not a model. The sector map and the India column of the scenario table are directional calls. The Brent ranges are indicative, not forecasts.
  • Rotation into India. The idea that money trimmed from crowded AI positions could move to cheaper markets is stated as a hypothesis. No flow data supports it yet.

What I would do next

Mark the note against what happened: the ceasefire curve, Brent, the rupee and foreign flows since 3 November, scenario by scenario.

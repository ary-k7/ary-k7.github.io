Amara Raja: a buy case, a sell case and a narrow call

The question

Amara Raja Energy & Mobility traded at its lowest price-to-book in ten years. I had to decide whether it was cheap for a reason or a strong franchise at a temporary low. This was my individual midterm for the Applied Value Investing course, submitted on 14 Oct 2026.

My call was Buy, with a three-year target 43% above the price on 13 Oct 2026: about 12.5% a year, plus a dividend yield of roughly 1.5%. I also wrote that it was a narrow call and the position should be small.

What I did

  • Ten years of financials, a five-forces review of the industry, and a comparison of return on tangible capital with Exide, the market leader.
  • Wrote the buy case and the sell case separately, each with its own forecasts and values from several methods, including a DCF, a reverse DCF and exit multiples.
  • Made twelve calls along the trade: battery dealers (including a competitor’s dealer), an inverter retailer, two mechanics, a scrap recycler, a company employee, and people in carmaker and EV-cell roles. All asked not to be named.
  • Checked the dealers’ claim of price increases against a year of online price histories.

What I found

Measure Amara Raja 10-year median
Price to book 1.7x 3.2x
P/E before one-offs 17.3x 21.3x
EV/EBITDA 8.3x 10.8x
  • Both Amara Raja and Exide earned above 20% on tangible capital in nine of the last ten years. Amara Raja’s share of the two leaders’ combined sales rose from 40.5% in FY16 to 44.4% in FY26.
  • The buy case is worth 71% more than the price in three years; the sell case is 43% less. I weighted them 75:25.
  • The 75% rests on the calls. Dealers said the price increases held and Exide followed within two weeks, and one dealer reported roughly half Exide’s warranty claims.

The weakness is in the report itself: on price alone, the sell case is stronger. My own buy-case DCF is only 5% above the market price, and the reverse DCF shows the price already assumes a margin recovery.

What would break this

  • The margin. The thesis needs the lead-acid margin to recover. It was 10.1% in Q1 FY27, and I set a sell trigger if it is still below 12% in the second half of FY27.
  • The weight. At a 60% weight on the buy case, the expected value falls short of a 12.5% annual return. The 75% is my judgement and cannot be calculated.
  • Governance. Family pay is about 14% of profit and half of capital spending goes through family companies. I argued this justifies a lower multiple rather than avoiding the stock; a sceptic can fairly disagree.
  • Lithium spending. A further sell trigger is the board committing capital to cells beyond its stated cap without customer contracts.
  • Evidence quality. Twelve conversations are anecdotes, not a survey, and I could not verify what I was told.

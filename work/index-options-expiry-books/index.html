Index-options expiry-day books and the combined book

Rules-based option-selling books on Nifty, Sensex and BankNifty expiry days, backtested minute by minute over five years and combined in one portfolio view.

The question

Is there a repeatable edge in selling index options on expiry day and the day before, and can several such books sit in one portfolio without stacking the same risk?

The backtests say yes, but costs and jump days decide how much of the edge is left.

What I did

  • Built six books on Nifty, Sensex and BankNifty (0DTE, 1DTE and positional) from a few families: short strangles and straddles with premium stops, decay-triggered entries, and a small option-buying sleeve.
  • Backtested on a 1-minute options store merged from broker data. The Nifty 0DTE book covers 281 expiries, Jul 2021 to Nov 2026.
  • I set the targets, constraints and acceptance rules; AI coding agents wrote most of the code and ran the searches.
  • Stress-tested the books with synthetic 1–2% index jumps, block-bootstrap drawdowns, slippage sensitivity, train/validation/holdout splits and margin-capped lot sizing.
  • Combined five books (four index books and a crude-oil expiry book) into one view. A clash rule lets only one index book trade on a given day; the crude book runs alongside them.

What I found

Nifty 0DTE book, backtest, 281 expiries (Jul 2021 – Nov 2026), statutory charges, no slippage, hedge wings excluded:

Measure Value
Win rate 82.6%
Monthly Sharpe 5.35
Max drawdown, as a share of average annual net 13%
Worst synthetic shock day, as a share of average annual net 12%
Average annual net, as a share of peak margin on one broker’s margin table 13%

That Sharpe describes a cost-free world, and the drawdown and shock ratios above divide by the same cost-free net, so they flatter the book too. The book generates about 32 orders a day in the backtest, and on the earlier v3 version (266 expiries, Jul 2021 – Jul 2026) 0.5 point of slippage per fill removes about 36% of annual net; 1 point removes about 72%.

The combined book (backtest, Jun 2021 – Nov 2026, statutory charges, no slippage on the index books) has a monthly Sharpe of 5.76, under the same caveats, and loses in 11.4% of weeks. Weekly correlations between the index books run from −0.10 to +0.11 (monthly, up to +0.24). Against crude, on fewer than 30 shared weeks, they range from −0.37 (Nifty 0DTE) to +0.47 (Nifty 1DTE).

The shares do not sum to 100%: removing a book hands its clash days to the next book in the order.

Removing Sensex 0DTE makes the combined maximum drawdown about 30% deeper.

In late October I found that the simulator’s range-breakout tracker started one minute late. Fixing it made the Nifty 0DTE maximum drawdown about 22% deeper; I kept the fix.

What would break this

  • Costs. One point of slippage per fill takes most of the edge.
  • The tail. A Sharpe above 5 on short premium mostly says the sample has not shown the bad day. The shock test is a model, and the backtests leave out the far out-of-the-money hedge wings the strategies are specified with.
  • Selection. The current Nifty 0DTE version was picked from 160 variants after the holdout had been opened, so it stays “not confirmed” until it passes a 13-expiry forward check. The Sensex 0DTE holdout has been looked at in four rounds, so its own notes discount the backtest by 16–23%. The BankNifty holdout result fell by a third after the engine fix, on seven expiries: too few to confirm or reject the edge.
  • Regime change. Expiry settlement moved to a closing auction in Aug 2026. Only 15 expiries exist under it, and the at-the-money straddle now holds about 35% of its extrinsic value at 15:15, against 2% before.
  • Margin. One broker snapshot with about 1% headroom; the combined view uses a flat margin model.

What I would do next

Replace the assumed slippage with measured bid-ask costs, add the hedge wings to the simulator, and change nothing until the forward checks are in.

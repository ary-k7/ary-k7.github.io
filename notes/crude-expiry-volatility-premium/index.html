Crude on expiry evening: the premium is real, and unhedged you still give it back

Across 46 MCX crude-oil expiries, realised volatility in the evening session ran below implied on 89% of days, yet only a delta-hedged short straddle kept a statistically significant share of the premium (backtest, Jan 2023 to Oct 2026, net of assumed slippage).

  • Volatility
  • Commodities
  • Options

I compared what crude-oil options priced in with what crude then did, over the evening session of MCX expiry days. The sample is 46 expiries from January 2023 to October 2026, on one-minute futures and options prices. Implied volatility comes from Black-76 on the at-the-money legs; realised volatility from one-minute futures returns to the close.

  • Realised volatility was a median 0.81 times implied, and below implied on 89% of expiries.
  • The variance premium averaged 31% (t-statistic 7.8) and was positive in every year from 2023 to 2026.

Collecting it is a separate question. In a backtest on the same 46 expiries, net of assumed slippage of 0.3 points per option fill and 1 point per futures trade:

  • A short at-the-money straddle, delta-hedged with futures every five minutes, kept 12.0% of the premium (t 3.7). It won on 72% of expiries, and its worst day lost 39% of the premium.
  • The same straddle without a hedge kept 6.5% (t 0.7), with a standard deviation of 66% of premium and a worst day of −142%.

The premium is real, but unhedged it is swamped by direction. The hedged result is also thin: with double the slippage it fell to about 4.5% of premium. Prices are last trades rather than bid and ask, and 46 expiries is a small sample.

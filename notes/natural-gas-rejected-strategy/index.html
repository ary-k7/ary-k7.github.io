Natural gas: great backtest, failed holdout, binned

The hedged expiry-day straddle that keeps part of the premium on Nifty loses money on natural gas, and a gas selling book that passed training and validation failed its holdout.

  • Volatility
  • Commodities
  • Research process

I ran the same scripts over the last six hours of 159 Nifty weekly and monthly expiries (November 2023 to November 2026) and 46 MCX natural-gas expiries (January 2023 to October 2026).

Gas has little premium to sell: realised volatility ran at 0.88 to 0.90 times implied, against 0.79 to 0.86 on Nifty. It is also costly to trade: one tick on all four fills of a straddle is 3.85% of the credit, against 0.18% on Nifty. In a backtest after hedging costs and base slippage (0.5 points per fill on Nifty, 0.05 on gas), a delta-hedged straddle kept 12.2% of the credit on Nifty (t 3.7) and lost 6.3% on gas (t −1.4).

On 10 November an agent-run search I had specified looked for a gas selling book (backtest on the same 46 expiries, split 22 / 12 / 12 into training, validation and holdout; statutory charges and 0.05 points of slippage per fill).

  • Of 3,264 configurations tested on the training set, only 31 had a Sharpe above 1.
  • The selected book met its target on training and validation (Sharpe 2.55 and 2.50), then failed the holdout (Sharpe −0.07). One high-volatility expiry in January 2026 used nearly the whole drawdown budget.
  • At 0.10 points of slippage, training and validation Sharpe fell to 1.40 and 0.88.
  • The deflated Sharpe ratio across all trials in the family was 0.02.

The verdict was overfit and not tradeable, and the search stopped: any change made after seeing the holdout would be fitted to it.

The book

I started as a vanilla thetabanger: sell index options on expiry day, go home. One strategy is one way to blow up, so now, thanks to AI, I can lose money faster across more avenues xD

  1. 01

    Expiry-day index options

    Nifty, Sensex, BankNiftyIntradayRead the research

    Where it started. Sell premium on expiry day, manage the stops, flat by the close. Theta pays the bills, right up until gamma sends one. Worked like a charm until SEBI chose to spoil the party :(

    ↗
  2. 02

    Non-expiry days in index options

    Nifty, SensexIntraday to overnightRead the research

    Added because expiry comes just twice a week and it made me look unemployed the other three days. Slower theta, kinder gamma, and the month stops hanging on a single session.

    ↗
  3. 03

    Positional weekly options

    Index weekliesDaysRead the research

    Added so I stop living and dying by the last day. Carrying positions through the week keeps me out of expiry-day gamma blasts. The price is overnight gap risk, which at least is a different way to lose.

    ↗
  4. 04

    Momentum in stock futures

    Large-cap stock futuresMonthlyRead the research

    It makes money when markets trend, which is exactly when premium sellers get run over. More importantly, it keeps my FOMO under control :p

    ↗
  5. 05

    Pair trades in stock futures

    Same-sector rivalsWeeks to monthsRead the research

    Long one stock, short its rival. Market-neutral, so I get to have an opinion without having one on broader markets. It takes the index out of the book altogether.

    The same expiry-day idea on a different underlying, in a different session, against a different crowd. Gives a different high trading through US market hours.

04

Stuck on

Things I have not figured out. If you have, tell me and save me a few months and many more tokens :p

  • 04.1

    Volatility forecasting that beats the obvious

    Every sizing decision in a short-vol book is a volatility forecast in disguise. I have GARCH-family models and implied vol itself as the benchmark, and beating implied out of sample is harder than the papers make it look. I still do not have a forecast I would size off without a discretionary override.

  • 04.2

    From rule-based to dynamic algorithms

    Everything I run is rules: fixed entries, fixed stops, fixed sizes. Markets are not fixed. I want strategies that adapt their parameters to the regime, without it turning into curve-fitting with extra steps. Where the line sits between adaptive and overfit is the part I have not cracked.

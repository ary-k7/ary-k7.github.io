A rules-based momentum rotation beat Nifty 50 buy&hold on both return and risk-adjusted return across every parameter combination tested. The chosen configuration — 9-month lookback, 6-month rebalance, top 20 equal-weight — delivered roughly 2.4× the index return at ~1.7× the Sharpe, sits on a broad robust plateau, and rebalances often enough to give many decision points.

02Strategy definition — every parameter

The strategy is fully specified by the parameters below. Two of them (lookback and rebalance period) were swept to find the best combination; the rest were held fixed throughout.

Universe100 stocks — the current Nifty100 constituents. Each has 1-minute OHLCV history from Dhan; daily close is the last traded price of the IST session.
Momentum signal (lookback)Trailing total price return over the last L months: close(t−1) / close(t−1−L months) − 1. Pure price momentum — no earnings, valuation or fundamentals. Chosen L = 9 (swept over {3, 6, 9, 12}).
RankingAll eligible stocks sorted in descending order of the lookback return (strongest performer first).
Selection (top N)The top 20 ranked stocks are held. The other 80 are ignored. Fixed at N = 20.
WeightingEqual weight — 5% per name (5% × 20 = 100%). Long-only, fully invested, no cash, no leverage, no shorting.
Rebalance periodPortfolio is re-ranked and reconstituted every R months. Between rebalances holdings are untouched and weights drift with price. Chosen R = 6 (swept over {3, 6, 9, 12}), anchored to Jan 1 / Jul 1.
ExecutionTrades occur on the first trading day of the rebalance month at that day's close. Names leaving the top 20 are sold; new entrants bought; survivors trimmed/topped back to 5%.
Transaction cost15 bps per side, charged on the notional that actually changes hands at each rebalance (models brokerage + slippage, flat).
BenchmarksNifty 50 and Nifty 500 buy & hold, rebased to the same start date as the strategy.
Evaluation windowJul 2018 – Jun 2026 for the parameter study — the common window where even a 12-month lookback has full history, so all configs are compared apples-to-apples.
MetricsCAGR (annualised), annualised Sharpe (daily returns, 0% risk-free), max drawdown (peak-to-trough on the daily equity curve), semi-annual period returns.

05Step 3 — is the edge real or lucky?

A long rebalance period gives few trading decisions over 8 years, so a peak could be an artifact of which months we rebalance on. We stress-tested the strong 6–9-month region two ways.

06The chosen strategy

9-month lookback · top 20 · equal-weight · 6-month rebalance

  • On the rebalance day, rank all 100 Nifty100 stocks by their trailing 9-month return.
  • Buy the top 20, 5% each (fully invested, long-only, no leverage).
  • Hold untouched for 6 months (rebalances anchored to Jan 1 / Jul 1), then re-rank and reconstitute.
  • Trade at the first session's close; budget 15 bps per side of turnover.

Chosen from the robust interior plateau: the 9-month lookback row is the steadiest in the grid (Sharpe 1.00–1.18, drawdowns −26% to −30%), and a 6-month rebalance gives far more decision points than the 9m/12m columns — a better balance of performance, robustness and out-of-sample trustworthiness than the raw peak cell.

08Caveats — read before trusting the absolute numbers

Survivorship / look-ahead bias is the big one. The universe is today's Nifty100, not point-in-time constituents. Stocks that fell out of the index are excluded and graduating winners are baked in, which inflates the strategy's returns (but not the index columns). The relative findings (momentum beats the index; longer lookback + moderate rebalance is the robust region) are trustworthy; the absolute CAGR is optimistic versus a true point-in-time universe.
  • No point-in-time membership or delisting handling — index reconstitution is ignored.
  • Close-to-close fills at a single price; real liquidity, impact and intraday slippage beyond the flat 15 bps are not modelled.
  • Sparse decisions at long horizons — a 6-month rebalance is only ~16 decisions in 8 years; Test B mitigates but cannot eliminate small-sample noise.
  • Single market regime — one ~9-year Indian bull-biased sample.
  • Concentration risk — momentum piles into hot themes (energy/PSU/metals recently), so the book turns sharply when leadership reverses (see the flat/negative 2025 halves).

Recommended next step: re-run the chosen config on a point-in-time Nifty100 membership list to size the survivorship haircut, and add a turnover/number-of-trades check for cost realism.

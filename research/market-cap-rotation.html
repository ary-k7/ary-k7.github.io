Market-Cap Momentum Rotation

Beating the Nifty 500 by rotating across Large / Mid / Small caps with a cash risk-off sleeve
Prepared 29 June 2026  ·  Data: Dhan v2 (NSE index history)  ·  Backtest window: Jan 2006 – Jun 2026  ·  Research note (not investment advice)
Contents:  1. Summary  ·  2. Strategy Definitions & Naming  ·  3. Recommended Strategy  ·  4. Results  ·  5. Robustness  ·  6. Risks  ·  7. Assumptions  ·  8. Next steps

1. Executive Summary

Objective: systematically beat the Nifty 500 by allocating across market-cap segments (large/mid/small) using price momentum, with a cash sleeve for risk-off periods.

Headline (2007–2026, 233 months): the recommended strategy — hold the strongest cap on 3-month momentum, move to cash when all caps are falling — delivered 18.8% CAGR vs the Nifty 500's 10.4%, and cut maximum drawdown from –61% to –25%. ₹1 grew to ₹28.1 vs ₹6.8 for the benchmark.

Is it real or curve-fit? It survived an honest walk-forward (parameters chosen using only past data) that captured ~95% of the edge out-of-sample, and the same idea independently ranked #1–#2 on a different small-cap index over a different window. It is robust to transaction costs and the pre-2011 cash-rate assumption.
The honest catch: the edge is primarily drawdown control, not return generation. Most of the advantage comes from avoiding crashes (2008, 2020) and riding trends; it underperforms in choppy, sideways bear markets (e.g. 2018–19) and beats the benchmark in only ~60% of rolling 12-month windows. All returns are gross of tax — monthly rotation has material tax consequences (see §7).

2. Strategy Definitions & Naming

Every strategy is a simple monthly rule. First, three shared concepts used by all of them:

  • Momentum score — the trailing return of a cap index over the last L months. A cap is "rising" if its score is positive.
  • Lookback L — how many months of history the score uses. We tested L = 3, 6, 9, 12; the recommended setting is L = 3 (most responsive).
  • Risk-off — the situation where all three caps have a negative momentum score (everything is falling). Strategies differ mainly in what they do here.
  • Rebalance — decisions are made on the last trading day of each month and held for the next month.

Below, each strategy is given an intuitive name (used in this report) alongside its original engine code-name (as it appears in the backtest files), a plain definition, and its risk-off behaviour.

Note: the long backtest uses the Nifty Smallcap 50 index as the small-cap proxy (Dhan carries it to 2006; Smallcap 250 only from 2019). Trade the Smallcap 250 ETF live — the two behave similarly.

3.2 Rules (the "Leader → Cash" strategy)

  1. Signal (monthly): on the last trading day of each month, compute the trailing 3-month return of Nifty 100, Nifty Midcap 150 and Nifty Smallcap 250.
  2. Selection: identify the cap with the highest 3-month return.
  3. Allocation: if that best return is positive, hold 100% of that cap's ETF. If all three are negative, hold 100% LIQUIDBEES.
  4. Rebalance: execute at/near the month-end close; hold until the next month-end. Turnover is low — often no change month-to-month.
Recommended enhancement before going live: rather than betting on exactly a 3-month lookback (a performance spike, not a stable plateau), average the signal across 2-, 3- and 4-month lookbacks (a lookback ensemble). This keeps most of the edge while reducing single-parameter sensitivity.

5.2 Independent corroboration & sensitivity

  • Cross-instrument: on the separate Nifty Smallcap 250 index (2020–2026), Blend→Cash ranked #1 (Sharpe 1.48) and Leader→Cash #2 (Sharpe 1.38) of 20 strategies — the idea wins on data it was not tuned on.
  • Transaction costs: CAGR moves only 18.9% → 18.3% as cost rises from 5 to 25 bps per switch (low turnover).
  • Pre-2011 cash proxy: varying the assumed 2007–2010 cash rate across 4% / 6% / 8% changes CAGR by only ±0.17% — immaterial.

6. Risks

Concentration risk. The strategy holds 100% in a single asset class at a time. No diversification within the equity sleeve — a sharp reversal in the held cap is taken in full.
Whipsaw / sideways-market risk. In trendless markets the signal flips in and out, buying late and selling late. Its worst environment — lost 23% vs benchmark in 2018–19 and 9% in 2022–23.
Tail-dependent edge. Beats the benchmark only ~60% of rolling years; much of the outperformance is concentrated in a few crash-avoidance and trend episodes. Fewer sharp crashes ahead would compress the edge.
Parameter fragility. The return peak sits specifically at the 3-month lookback and does not smoothly persist at 6/9/12 months (spike, not plateau). Mitigate with the lookback ensemble.
Still an equity strategy. Max drawdown ~25% — better than the benchmark's 61%, but far from capital-protected.
Regime dependence of the cap tilt. Part of the historical advantage came from mid/small caps outperforming large; over a full cycle this premium is smaller and can reverse for years.
Small sample. ~19 years, one country, monthly decisions — few independent observations. Treat the statistics as a fragility screen, not proof of a persistent anomaly.
Execution & liquidity risk. Midcap and especially smallcap ETFs can trade at wider spreads / premiums to NAV; large orders may face impact cost beyond the modelled 10 bps.

7. Assumptions & Caveats (Flags)

These materially affect how the backtested numbers translate to a live, after-cost, after-tax account.

⚑ Taxes are NOT modelled — the biggest gap. Monthly rotation realises gains on holdings held well under a year, so most are taxed as short-term capital gains (15% in India, 20% from Jul 2024). On a taxable account this is a large drag and would meaningfully reduce the net edge. Consider a tax-aware variant (longer minimum holding / quarterly rebalance).
⚑ Backtested on indices, not ETFs. Index returns exclude ETF expense ratios (~0.1–0.5%/yr), tracking error, bid-ask spreads and market impact beyond the flat 10 bps switch cost. A live ETF implementation will lag.
⚑ Price-return, not total-return, indices. The equity legs use price indices, so dividends (~1–1.5%/yr) are excluded. The benchmark is also price-return, so the relative comparison is roughly fair, but absolute CAGRs understate dividend-inclusive ETF returns. The cash leg (Nifty 1D Rate) is total-return.
⚑ Small-cap proxy for deep history. 2006–2018 small-cap data uses the Nifty Smallcap 50 index (50 stocks, more concentrated/volatile) because Smallcap 250 history on Dhan begins only 2019. The 2019+ overlap shows the same strategy ranking, but long-run figures inherit the proxy's character.
⚑ Pre-2011 cash-rate proxy. The Nifty 1D Rate index starts Jan 2011; for 2007–2010 a flat 6%/yr overnight rate was assumed. Sensitivity: ±0.17% CAGR (immaterial).
⚑ Execution timing. Signals computed at month-end close and assumed executed at that close with no slippage/lag beyond 10 bps and guaranteed fills. Real execution occurs slightly after the signal.
⚑ No look-ahead (verified). Each month's signal uses only data up to the prior month-end; returns are realised the following month.

8. Recommended Steps Before Going Live

  1. Build the lookback ensemble (blend 2/3/4-month signals) and re-confirm the walk-forward holds.
  2. Translate to ETFs and measure realised tracking error, spreads and liquidity for the chosen midcap/smallcap ETFs.
  3. Run an after-tax analysis (STCG-aware); test whether quarterly rebalance or a minimum-holding rule improves net-of-tax returns.
  4. Paper-trade for 1–2 quarters to validate signal generation, execution timing and turnover before committing capital.

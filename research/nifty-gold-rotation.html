NIFTYBEES / GOLDBEES Rotation — Backtest Results

Daily ETF data via Dhan · Backtest window from the 2008 crash bottom (2008-11-03) to 2026-06-25 · Prepared 2026-06-28

1. Overview

This report compares five ways to allocate capital between two NSE ETFs — NIFTYBEES (Nifty 50) and GOLDBEES (gold) — with an optional cash leg. The active strategies aim to beat a simple buy-and-hold of either ETF on a risk-adjusted basis by rotating into whichever asset is trending.

All results are gross of costs (the switching cost of ~0.001% is negligible), measured close-to-close, with positions taken at each day’s close and held forward, i.e. no look-ahead. The window starts at the 2008 crash bottom, which flatters every entry — see Caveats.

2. Instruments & Data

  • NIFTYBEES (securityId 10576) — Nifty 50 ETF. Daily OHLCV from Dhan, 2008–present.
  • GOLDBEES (securityId 14428) — gold ETF. Daily OHLCV from Dhan, 2008–present.
  • Cash — proxied by the Nifty 1D Rate Index (overnight rate). Real index from 2011-01-03; 2008–2010 backfilled with a stepped overnight-rate proxy (2008 ≈ 7.0%, 2009 ≈ 3.5%, 2010 ≈ 5.0% annualized).
Why not LIQUIDBEES for cash? LIQUIDBEES was downloaded, but its price is fixed at ~₹1000 (returns are paid out as dividends, not price appreciation), so its price return is ~0%. The Nifty 1D Rate Index is used instead as a realistic cash-return proxy.

3. Strategy Definitions — exactly how each is built

Common conventions for all strategies: signals use daily closing prices; the chosen position is entered at that day’s close and earns the next day’s close-to-close return (no look-ahead); the portfolio is always 100% in a single instrument (no blending, no leverage).

A. Method 2 — 3-asset Relative Strength rotates: Nifty / Gold / Cash

Universe: NIFTYBEES, GOLDBEES, Cash (Nifty 1D Rate index).
Signal: each day compute the trailing 21-day total return (the “momentum score”) of all three: mom = close[t] / close[t-21] − 1.
Rule: hold 100% in the single asset with the highest momentum score. Cash wins the ranking automatically when both ETFs’ 21-day momentum falls below the cash accrual — i.e. during sustained joint declines the strategy parks in cash.
Lookback: L = 21 trading days (chosen from a sweep of 21 / 63 / 126 / 252; shorter was best on this data).

B. EMA(3,10) — 2-asset Ratio Rotation rotates: Nifty / Gold

Universe: NIFTYBEES, GOLDBEES only (no cash).
Signal: form the price ratio R = NIFTYBEES_close / GOLDBEES_close, then take two exponential moving averages of that ratio: EMA(3) and EMA(10).
Rule: if EMA3(R) > EMA10(R) (the ratio is trending up ⇒ Nifty outperforming) hold 100% NIFTYBEES; otherwise hold 100% GOLDBEES.
Intuition: a fast trend-following cross on the relative strength of equity vs gold.

C. SMA(5,20) — 2-asset Ratio Rotation rotates: Nifty / Gold

Universe: NIFTYBEES, GOLDBEES only (no cash).
Signal: same ratio R = NIFTYBEES_close / GOLDBEES_close, but with simple moving averages SMA(5) and SMA(20).
Rule: if SMA5(R) > SMA20(R) hold 100% NIFTYBEES; otherwise hold 100% GOLDBEES. This is the original baseline signal; EMA(3,10) is its faster variant.

D. NIFTYBEES — Buy & Hold benchmark

Buy the Nifty 50 ETF on day one and hold it unchanged for the entire window. No signal, no switching.

E. GOLDBEES — Buy & Hold benchmark

Buy the gold ETF on day one and hold it unchanged for the entire window. No signal, no switching.

5. Key Findings

  • Method 2 (3-asset) and EMA(3,10) are effectively tied on growth (20.5% vs 20.1% CAGR) — both roughly triple the CAGR of holding either ETF.
  • Method 2 is the best risk-adjusted vehicle — highest Sharpe (1.33) and lowest volatility (15.1%) — because the cash leg (held 18% of the time) smooths the ride.
  • EMA(3,10) has the shallowest drawdown (−23.7%) despite holding no cash, so the cash leg’s benefit here is mostly lower volatility rather than crash protection.
  • The faster EMA(3,10) beats the slower SMA(5,20) on both return and drawdown; with negligible costs its higher turnover is not penalized.
  • No strategy dominates year-by-year — the rotation strategies win by compounding (avoiding disaster years such as Nifty’s −24% in 2011) rather than topping the table every year.

7. Caveats & Limitations

  • Best-case entry. Starting at the 2008 crash bottom inflates every CAGR; it skips the ~50% decline that preceded it. Treat these as upper-bound figures, not typical entries.
  • Costs excluded. Brokerage is negligible, but real-world slippage and the inability to trade exactly at the official close would tax high-turnover strategies (Method 2: ~32 switches/yr) more than low-turnover ones.
  • Cash backfill is approximate. 2008–2010 overnight returns are a stepped proxy, not the actual index (which began in 2011).
  • Price-return only. ETF dividends (notable for NIFTYBEES) are excluded, modestly understating equity-heavy strategies.
  • Single historical path. One 17.6-year sample; parameters (lookback, MA spans) were chosen with hindsight and may not persist out-of-sample.
